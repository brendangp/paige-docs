Billing in Paige is managed at the project level. Each project has its own subscription plan, and AI usage — such as the AI agent writing and modifying your bot code — is charged against a credits balance. Payments are processed securely through Paystack.

## Free trial

Every new project starts on a **14-day free trial** with full access to every paid feature — the **Conversations** tab, **Broadcasts**, the **Deploy** button, and bot runtime on your connected WhatsApp number. You can build, deploy, and run your bot end-to-end during the trial without entering payment details.

When the trial ends and you have not subscribed:

- **Locked.** The Conversations tab, Broadcasts tab, Deploy button, and bot runtime are gated until you subscribe. Inbound WhatsApp messages on your connected number are received but the bot will not reply.
- **Still open.** The **Bot** tab, the AI chat sidebar, every Settings section (including **WhatsApp**), the in-app preview, and the Paige Dev testing number remain available — so you can keep iterating and connect or re-configure your WhatsApp number whenever you're ready.

Subscribe for **R570/month** from **Settings → Billing** to lift the gate immediately. Your bot resumes responding on your real WhatsApp number from the moment your subscription becomes active.

<Tip>
  You can connect your WhatsApp Business number through Meta embedded signup at any point — during the trial, after the trial expires, or after you subscribe. The connection itself is never gated.
</Tip>

## The subscription

Inside the app there is one subscription to buy: **R570 a month**, per project. If you run several projects, each one has its own subscription.

Subscribe from **Settings → Billing** — click **Subscribe — R570/mo** and pay through Paystack. Paige keeps your card on file and charges it again every 30 days.

<Note>
  **Update card also charges R570.** It isn't a small verification charge — updating your card runs a full payment, which also settles anything overdue in the same transaction. Use it when your card has genuinely changed, not to check the details are right.
</Note>

<Note>
  Billing doesn't apply to self-hosted projects — those run on your own infrastructure, so there's no platform fee. Self-hosted projects still buy credits, because they still use Paige's AI agents.
</Note>

## Who can manage billing

Only the **project owner** or a **workspace owner or admin** can manage billing. That covers subscribing, updating the card, pausing, resuming and cancelling, buying credits, changing your billing details and setting the Low Credit Alert. On a personal project, that's you. On a [workspace](/guides/workspaces) project, it's the workspace owner and its admins.

The **Billing** and **Usage** sections only appear in Settings for those people. Everyone else on the project still uses credits when they chat with Paige; they just can't buy credits or change the subscription. If they try, Paige tells them *Only the project owner or a workspace admin can do this*. When credits run low or run out, they see a note above the chat box asking them to have the project owner or a workspace admin top up.

## Starter and Managed

There are three ways to get a Paige bot, and only one of them is a checkout.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Starter" icon="rocket">
    You build it yourself. Sign up, build with the Code Agent, and subscribe at **R570 a month** per project when the trial ends. Everything else on this page describes Starter.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Managed" icon="handshake">
    Green Pages builds it for you. You pay a **R8,000 once-off setup fee** per project for the build, then an **agreed monthly amount** if you want Green Pages to keep running it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Enterprise" icon="building-2">
    For deployments that have to run on your own infrastructure and meet your own security and compliance requirements. Quoted per organisation.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**Managed is a conversation, not a checkout.** There is no Managed button in **Settings → Billing**. The setup fee is **R8,000 per project**; the ongoing monthly is agreed when the build is finished, because it depends on what Green Pages runs for you. Email [info@greenpages.app](mailto:info@greenpages.app) and Green Pages scopes it with you.

When a Managed build is finished, you choose what happens next:

- **Take it over yourself.** The project becomes an ordinary Starter project at **R570 a month**, and you carry on building in Paige like anyone else.
- **Leave it with us.** Green Pages keeps running it, broadcasts and campaigns included, for the monthly amount you agreed.

<Note>
  All three run on the same Paige platform with the same controls. Managed buys you our time; Enterprise buys you control over where it runs and the evidence your organisation needs. **Enterprise is not more secure than Starter or Managed** — nobody on a smaller plan is missing protection.
</Note>

### Enterprise

Enterprise answers a different question again: not "can you build it" but **where does it run, and what does it have to comply with**. It covers deployment on your own hosted infrastructure or infrastructure you nominate, single sign-on and access control set to your requirements, and the compliance reviews, security questionnaires and signed agreements a larger organisation works through before it can buy anything. Custom builds and integrations sit here too. It is quoted rather than priced, and it starts with a conversation.

<Tip>
  Paige missing something you need, rather than needing a build? [Request a feature](/guides/request-a-feature) — that reaches the same team and costs nothing.
</Tip>

## Two different things you pay for

This trips people up, so it's worth separating clearly. **Your subscription and your credits are unrelated**, and they buy different things.

| | Subscription | Credits |
|---|---|---|
| **What it buys** | Your bot running live — conversations, broadcasts, deploys | Paige's AI agents doing work for you |
| **Cost** | R570/month per project | R19 per credit, bought when you want them |
| **Lives in** | Settings → Billing | Settings → Usage |

So a project with a subscription and no credits has a bot that runs perfectly and an AI you can't ask to change it. A project with credits and no subscription is one you can keep building on, but whose bot has stopped talking to customers.

## AI credits

Credits pay for the AI agents inside Paige — the Code agent that builds your bot, the Conversations agent that helps you reply, the Broadcasting agent that plans campaigns, and the Help Agent that answers your questions. The more work you ask of them, the more they use.

**Every new project gets 5 free credits** to start with.

Your balance, about how many prompts it still buys, and your full history are in **Settings → Usage**. See [The Usage page](#the-usage-page).

<Note>
  **Every prompt costs credits, questions included.** That covers questions to the [Help Agent](/agents/help-agent), and a request Paige turns down. A small question costs very little, but it isn't free.
</Note>

<Tip>
  The [AI agents overview](/agents/overview) explains why the AI running inside your deployed bot never shows up here.
</Tip>

Credits don't pay for your bot talking to customers, deploys, or WhatsApp's own message costs. Those come from your subscription and from Meta respectively — [Message Costs](/guides/message-costs) prices what Meta charges you.

### What one prompt costs

Each message you send to an agent is one **prompt**, and one prompt can use several AI models behind the scenes: the agent itself, a flow builder or test it starts, a short summary of an older chat, the name it picks for a new project. You pay for all of them, each at its own model's price, as **one total for the prompt**.

- **While the agent works**, the line under the chat box reads **This reply so far: … credits** and keeps going up. When it finishes, the line reads **This reply: … credits**. That line covers the latest reply only. Credits leave your balance as the work happens.
- **In Settings → Usage**, each request is one row in the history, with its total. See [The Usage page](#the-usage-page).
- **If you press Stop**, nothing more is charged from that moment. The work the agent had already finished is still charged.
- **Describing an image you attach** uses credits too. That happens when you upload it, before your prompt starts, so it shows as its own **Image description** row.

### The Usage page

**Settings → Usage** shows your **Credit Balance** and, under it, about how many prompts it will still buy, for example **About 12 prompts left**. That's your balance divided by what your last 20 prompts cost on average, so treat it as an estimate: a big build uses more than a small question. A new project shows it after the first prompt. The balance turns amber below your Low Credit Alert level (1 credit if you haven't set one) and red at zero.

{/* TODO: screenshot of Settings → Usage: the Credit Balance card with "About N prompts left", and the history with one row expanded to its parts. Capture kit, not hand-shot. */}

The **History** below it has five columns: **Date**, **Prompt**, **Steps**, **Credits** and **Balance**. Choose **This month**, **Last 30 days** or **All time** to scope it.

- **One request, one row.** One thing you ask for can take more than one reply, for example when Paige asks you a question before it starts. Every reply is charged as it happens, and Usage shows them together as one row, labelled with what you first asked, with the total credits and steps. Tap **parts** on the row to see what each reply cost. A new request in the same chat is a new row.
- **Whose prompt it was.** You see the text of your own prompts, shortened to fit. A teammate's reads **Prompt by** and their name; their text is never shown to you. Work done through the API or MCP reads **API or MCP request**, and a top-up reads **Credits added**.
- **Marks on a row.** A request that's still working is marked **Running**, and its total goes up as it works. One you stopped is marked **Stopped**, and one that failed is marked **Failed**.
- **The Balance column** is what your balance really was just after that row's last part. If something else happened between a request's parts, such as a top-up, the column won't subtract neatly from one row to the next.

Older prompts, and prompts from before a project reset, show no text or steps.

### Message Costs: two checks that read your bot

Looking at **Tools → Message Costs** is free. Two buttons in it use credits, because Paige reads your bot with AI to answer them:

- **Estimate from my bot**, on the calculator's **Your bot** tab.
- **Find savings in my bot**, on the **Send fewer messages** card — and **Apply**, when you let Paige make the changes it found.

Each one tells you roughly how many credits it will use **before** you click, and shows how many it **actually used** afterwards. The savings check is switched off, with the reason, when the project doesn't have enough credits. All of them are charged even on a bring-your-own-key project, since your own key only covers your bot's replies.

The results are saved for the project and shared with your team, so nobody pays again just to look at them. See [The Your bot tab](/guides/message-costs#the-your-bot-tab) and [Send fewer messages](/guides/message-costs#send-fewer-messages).

<Note>
  **Why isn't the rand figure in Message Costs worked out at R19?** R19 is what Paige charges for a credit. Message Costs shows **Meta's** charge, which Meta bills in US dollars, so it converts at the day's market exchange rate instead, with nothing added. See [Rand or dollars](/guides/message-costs#rand-or-dollars).
</Note>

<Warning>
  **A request that fails is still charged for the AI work it did before it failed**, and asking the agent again is a new request. [Troubleshooting](/guides/troubleshooting#what-this-costs-you-in-credits) covers what that means in practice and how to keep a run that's going wrong from costing more.
</Warning>

### Documents: analysed once, sent free

[Document templates](/guides/documents) follow the same rule, and it's worth being explicit because the answer surprises people:

- **Paige analysing your template uses credits — once.** That's ordinary building work, and it shows in Settings → Usage like any other build. Analysing a second template, or re-analysing one you've changed, costs again.
- **Your bot filling that template in and sending it uses no credits at all**, however many documents it sends. There's no AI at that point — Paige already wrote the code, and the code just drops your values into the blanks.

<Warning>
  **This is still true if you bring your own AI key.** Your own key covers your bot's *conversations* — the AI that talks to your customers. Setting a template up is Paige building something for you, so it uses Paige credits like every other build. See [AI in your bot](/guides/ai-in-your-bot).
</Warning>

### Buy credits

Go to **Settings → Usage** and click **Buy Credits** on the Credit Balance card, or click **Buy credits** in the warning above the chat box. Pick 5, 10, 20, or 50 — or enter your own amount up to 500. Credits cost **R19 each** and land as soon as the payment clears.

<Tip>
  Set a **Low Credit Alert** on the same page and Paige emails you before you run out, rather than you finding out mid-sentence.
</Tip>

### Low-credit warning and zero-balance gating

Paige warns you in-app before your credits run out, and stops new AI requests cleanly when your balance hits zero.

- **Low-credit warning.** Once your balance drops below one credit, a banner appears above the chat input. If you can [manage billing](#who-can-manage-billing), it has a **Buy credits** button: the purchase opens right there, and the chat is ready again a few moments after you pay. Anyone else sees a note asking them to have the project owner or a workspace admin top up.
- **Zero-balance gate.** When your balance reaches zero, the chat won't start a new request on any agent, and that includes questions to the Help Agent. A request that is already running is allowed to finish, so your balance can end up slightly below zero.

<Info>
  The banner balance is refreshed after every message and after every top-up, so as soon as your credits land the chat unblocks automatically.
</Info>

## Green Pages Numbers are billed separately

If you rent a [WhatsApp number from Green Pages](/guides/numbers), that number has **its own subscription**. It is not part of your R570 project subscription and it doesn't use credits.

| | Paige subscription | A Green Pages number |
|---|---|---|
| **What it buys** | Your bot running live on one project | One WhatsApp-ready phone number |
| **Cost** | R570/month per project | R90/month per number |
| **Charged** | Every 30 days | First month up front, then every 30 days |
| **Belongs to** | The project | The person who bought the number |
| **Lives in** | Settings → Billing | Settings → Numbers |

- **Paying.** You pay the first month through Paystack when you buy the number, and the same card is charged every 30 days after that.
- **A failed payment.** Nothing is switched off straight away. You are emailed, and **Pay now** appears on the number in **Settings → Numbers**.
- **Cancelling.** **Cancel subscription** is on the number itself, behind **Manage**. Read [what happens when a subscription ends](/guides/numbers#what-happens-when-a-subscription-ends) first: the number is cancelled with the mobile network and cannot be recovered.
- **Invoices.** Every successful payment for a number gets a numbered invoice from the same sequence as your Paige invoices, for example `PAIGE-2026-0013`. It is made out to the person who bought the number and emailed to the address they sign in with. To download one, open **Payments** at the bottom of **Your numbers** in **Settings → Numbers** and click the invoice number.

<Note>
  Numbers is being rolled out gradually. If your Settings has no **Numbers** section, it isn't available on your account yet.
</Note>

## Pause, resume, and cancel

You can change the state of your subscription at any time from the **Billing** section.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Pause">
    Pausing your subscription temporarily suspends your project. Your bot stops handling messages, but your code and data are preserved. Click **Pause subscription** in the Billing section to pause.

    Everything is copied and kept safe first: your contacts, conversations and messages, your own database tables, and your **files** — the images and documents you uploaded, plus any photos, voice notes and documents your customers sent in. That last part matters, because it's what makes images still show in your chats and in [Storage](/guides/storage) when you come back, rather than returning as broken pictures.

    <Note>
      A paused project cannot receive or respond to WhatsApp messages until you resume.
    </Note>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Resume">
    To bring a paused project back online, click **Resume subscription** in the Billing section.

    Putting everything back takes a few moments. Your project shows what it's doing while it rebuilds, and your bot starts handling messages again as soon as it's finished — you can leave the page and come back.

    If any part doesn't come back cleanly, Paige tells you rather than pretending everything is fine: you get a message with a **Retry** button, and your saved copy is kept until the restore works. Retrying is always safe — nothing is duplicated, and nothing good is overwritten with a worse copy.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Cancel">
    Cancelling ends your subscription at the end of the current billing period. Click **Cancel subscription** and confirm. If you change your mind before the period ends, you can reactivate from the same section.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Who your invoices are made out to

**Billing details** is where you tell Paige who your invoices should be made out to:

- A **legal name** — your company’s registered name, or your own name.
- Your **address** — street, suburb, city and postal code.
- The **invoice email** invoices should go to.

The first time you open it, Paige fills in what it already knows — your workspace’s business name and address, or your own name for a personal project, and the email it already bills — so check it over and click **Save billing details**. The legal name and invoice email are required, and the form tells you straight away if the email doesn’t look right.

<Note>
  **These details belong to whoever pays, not to one project.** Every project in the same [workspace](/guides/workspaces), or every project you own personally, shares them — so you only enter them once.
</Note>

They’re kept separately from your workspace settings, so transferring workspace ownership never changes the invoice email you typed.

<Warning>
  Changes apply to **future invoices only**. An invoice that has already been issued keeps the details it was issued with — so fix your billing details *before* your next renewal, not after.
</Warning>

Paige does not charge VAT, so there is no VAT number field. Only the project owner or a workspace admin can change billing details.

## Payment history

Scroll to the bottom of **Settings → Billing** and you'll find **Payment History** — every charge on the project, with the date, what it was for (a subscription renewal or a credit purchase), the amount in rand, and whether it went through. Each successful charge also carries a **Download** button for its [invoice](#your-invoices).

<Note>
  The section only appears once you've actually paid for something. On a project still in its trial there's nothing there — not an empty table, but no heading at all. That's expected rather than a fault.
</Note>

Payment history is per project, so check each project's Billing section separately.

<Warning>
  If a payment fails, your project may be paused automatically. Update your payment method and reactivate your subscription from the **Billing** section to restore your bot.
</Warning>

## Your invoices

Every successful payment gets its own **numbered invoice** — for example `PAIGE-2026-0012`. You get it two ways: by email when you’re charged, and as a download whenever you want it.

### Invoices by email

Paige emails your invoice every time you’re charged — when you subscribe, when your monthly subscription renews automatically, when you reactivate a paused project, and when you buy credits.

It goes to the **invoice email** in your billing details. If you haven’t set one, it goes to the email Paige already bills: the project owner’s, or your workspace owner’s.

The email comes from Paige with the subject **Your Paige invoice** and the invoice number, shows the amount, the date and the project, and has the invoice attached as a **PDF**. Replying to it reaches the Green Pages team.

<Note>
  You get **one email per payment**, never duplicates. Invoices from before this feature started aren’t sent — download those instead.
</Note>

### Downloading an invoice

In **Payment History**, click **Download** next to a payment. The invoice saves to your computer or phone as a PDF named after its invoice number. On a phone each payment is its own card, with the **Download** button underneath.

You can download the same invoice as many times as you like — it always shows the details it was issued with.

A very long legal name, address or project name is shortened with an ellipsis (**…**) on the PDF so it fits the invoice. Your billing details themselves are saved in full.

- A payment that is still **pending**, **failed** or was **cancelled** has no invoice, so it has no Download button.
- A payment that has only just gone through may take a few minutes before its invoice appears.
- If a download doesn’t work, Paige shows a message at the bottom of the screen. Wait a moment and try again, and contact support if it keeps failing.

<Note>
  Didn’t get the email? Check your spam folder first — then download the same invoice here. This is separate from any receipt your card provider or Paystack sends you.
</Note>
