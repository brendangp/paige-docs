The **Conversations** tab is your inbox. On the left is the list of everyone who has messaged your bot; on the right is the selected conversation's full history. It's where you watch what your bot is doing, step in when a customer needs a person, and reply by hand.

For the underlying model — contacts, conversation state, handover, and the 24-hour window in depth — see [Conversations](/concepts/conversations). This page is about the tab itself and what its indicators mean.

## The contact list

Every contact who has messaged your bot appears in the left-hand list, most recent at the top, with a preview of the last message.

- **Search** the list by name or phone number with the box at the top.
- **Filter by state** with the **All states** dropdown to show only conversations in a particular [state](/concepts/conversations#conversation-state) — for example everyone currently in handover.

## What the green circle means

A **green ring around a contact's avatar** means their **24-hour messaging window is open** — they've sent you a message within the last 24 hours, so you can reply freely.

This matters because of a Meta rule, not a Paige one: a business can only send free-form messages to a customer for **24 hours** after that customer's last message. While the green ring is showing, you're inside that window. Once it's gone, the window has closed and you can only reach that contact with an approved [message template](/guides/templates).

### Three indicators, three meanings

The list uses three markers that are easy to mix up. They mean different things:

| Marker | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Green ring** around the avatar | Their **24-hour window is open** — you can reply freely right now. |
| Small **green badge with an eye** | A **preview/test contact** you created while testing — not a real customer. |
| Small **orange badge** | A conversation you've **taken over** in handover — your bot is paused for them. |

## Taking over from your bot

When a customer needs a person — they're upset, the request is unusual, or they've asked for one — click **Handover** at the top of the conversation. Your bot stops responding to that contact entirely; their messages still arrive and are recorded, but they wait for you.

<Warning>
  **You can't type to a customer until handover is on.** The reply box is disabled otherwise and tells you why: *Enable handover mode to send messages*. Turn on handover first.
</Warning>

To hand back, click **Handover ON** and pick the [state](/concepts/conversations#conversation-state) to resume from — your bot needs to know where in the conversation to pick up. The full mechanics, including why there's no plain "give it back" button, are on the [Conversations](/concepts/conversations#taking-over-from-your-bot) page.

### When the window has closed

If the 24-hour window has expired, the handover button reads **24h expired** and the reply box shows *24-hour messaging window has expired*. Your only way to reach the contact after that is an approved [message template](/guides/templates) — and you can send one without leaving this tab.

## Sending a template

Click the **Send template** button in the composer to open the picker. It works whether or not the window is open, and whether or not handover is on — unlike the reply box, it isn't gated, because a template is exactly what you need when everything else is unavailable.

The picker shows the templates Meta has already approved for your number. Pick one and you'll see it exactly as the customer will.

### Filling in the blanks

If the template has placeholders — `{{1}}`, `{{2}}` — you fill them in right there in the picker, and the preview updates as you type. You can't send until every placeholder has a value, so a customer never receives a message with a raw `{{1}}` in it.

### The conversation starter

Paige keeps one standard template, the **conversation starter**, pinned to the top of the picker. It's a plain one-tap opener whose only job is to get a reply — and a reply is what reopens the 24-hour window so you can talk normally again.

If your project doesn't have one yet, the picker offers to create it for you. Meta reviews it like any other template, which usually takes a few minutes.

<Warning>
  **Sending the starter takes the conversation over.** It switches the conversation into handover so you reply yourself and your bot stops answering, until you switch handover back off. There's no opt-out — the whole point of the starter is to begin a human conversation.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Sending a template costs you a Meta conversation charge, and templates are the one message type that can reach someone who hasn't written to you recently. Use them to restart a real conversation, not to broadcast — for that, use [Broadcasts](/guides/broadcasts), which is built for sending to many people at once.
</Note>

## Tagging a contact while you're talking to them

Tags are labels you put on a contact — `vip`, `interested`, `trial` — and they're what [segments](/guides/broadcasts/segments) and broadcasts use to find people later. You can add or remove them without leaving the conversation.

Open a conversation and click the **tag icon** in the thread header. It shows a number when the contact already has tags. On a phone it opens a panel from the bottom of the screen; on a computer it opens next to the button. Inside you'll find:

- The contact's current tags, each with a small **×** to take it off.
- A box to type in, and a list of every tag already used on your contacts with how many people carry each. Clicking one is usually what you want — it puts this person into an audience you already use.
- Anything you type that isn't in the list becomes a new tag.

Each change saves on its own as soon as you make it — there's no Save button. If something goes wrong the tag goes back to how it was and you'll see why. A contact can carry up to 25 tags.

**Capitals don't matter when you type.** Tags are stored in lower case, so `VIP` and `vip` are the same thing to type. Removing isn't fussy about capitals either: taking off `VIP` removes `vip` and `ViP` too, which is how you tidy up older tags.

<Warning>
  **Older tags with capitals are marked in the list.** If your contacts were tagged before Paige started saving in lower case, you may see a row like `VIP · 12` — a tag with a capital, on 12 contacts. Tags match exactly, so tagging someone from that row would *not* put them in with those 12.

  Paige marks that row in amber, says plainly that it won't join them, and doesn't add it in one click — it drops the name into the box so you can decide. To get everyone into one audience, rename `VIP` to `vip` in **Broadcasts → Contacts → Tags** and Paige moves all 12 across.
</Warning>

Tags added here work exactly like tags added anywhere else, so a segment built on `vip` picks this contact up straight away and your next broadcast to that segment reaches them.

## Let the Conversations Agent help

Open this tab and the chat sidebar switches to the **[Conversations Agent](/agents/conversations-agent)** — an assistant that knows your contacts and their history. Ask it to find contacts waiting in handover, summarise a conversation, or draft a reply for you to review and send.

You can also just ask it to tag someone: *"tag them as vip"*, *"mark this person as a lead"*, *"take the trial tag off them"*. It writes the change straight away and the tags in the thread header update as it does — there's no card to approve, because one contact is a small change you can undo with a tap. It handles **one contact at a time**; to tag a whole group, ask the [Broadcasting agent](/agents/broadcasting-agent) instead.
