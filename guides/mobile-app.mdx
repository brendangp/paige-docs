Paige works in your phone's browser, but it's much better installed to your home screen: it opens like a real app, and it can notify you when a customer needs a person.

## Install it

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Android">
    Open Paige in Chrome and you'll get a prompt: **Install Paige — Add Paige to your home screen for the full app experience.** Tap **Install**.

    Missed it or dismissed it? Open the drawer (☰) and look for **Install to home screen**, or use Chrome's menu → **Install app**.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="iPhone and iPad">
    Safari doesn't prompt, so you do it yourself: tap the **Share** button, then **Add to Home Screen**.

    <Warning>
      On iPhone this isn't optional if you want notifications. Apple only allows push notifications for apps added to the Home Screen — in a normal Safari tab the notification toggle is disabled and reads *"Not supported on this browser"*. Install first, then turn notifications on from the installed app. Requires iOS 16.4 or later.
    </Warning>

    Use Safari for this. Chrome and Firefox on iPhone can't add to the home screen.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Either way, you can tell it worked by what's missing: the installed app opens with no address bar and no browser toolbar, filling the screen the way a native app does.

## Getting around

Everything lives behind the **☰** menu in the top-left: **Build**, **Conversations**, **Flows**, **Broadcasts**, **Tools**, **Settings**, and **Help**. One screen at a time, rather than the three columns you get on a desktop.

The useful trick is **swiping**. Most tabs are a pair, and you swipe left and right between them:

| Tab | Swipe between |
|---|---|
| **Build** | the chat ↔ the phone preview |
| **Conversations** | your conversations ↔ the Conversations assistant |
| **Broadcasts** | your broadcasts ↔ the Broadcasting assistant |
| **Flows** | your flows ↔ the chat |

So on Build you describe a change, then swipe right to try it — which is most of what building a bot actually is.

Each of these is a full tab on the desktop, with its own page: the [Preview panel](/guides/preview-tab), the [Conversations inbox](/guides/conversations-tab), and [Settings](/guides/settings-project).

<Note>
  **Build** is the Code tab renamed. On a phone the front door is the chat, not the code.
</Note>

## What doesn't work on a phone

<Warning>
  **You can't edit code on mobile.** You can view your bot's file structure from the **Files** button in the Build chat, but tapping a file only shows you its path — there's no editor. Everything the AI writes is fine; it's hand-editing that's desktop-only.
</Warning>

Also worth knowing: the live preview when creating a [message template](/guides/templates) is hidden on phones, so you're composing without seeing it rendered. Templates are worth doing at a desk.

## Notifications

Notifications work on your computer as well as your phone, and Paige doesn't have to be installed, except on iPhone and iPad. Each person turns them on for themselves, on each device or browser they use, because only you can click **Allow**.

### Turning them on

You don't need Settings for this. There are three ways in:

- **The prompt.** If you have the Conversations tab on a project, a **Turn on notifications** message appears when you open that project. Click **Enable**, then **Allow** when your browser asks. Close it with the **X** and it won't come back on that device.
- **Your account menu.** Click your picture at the top right and choose **Turn on notifications**. On a phone it's in the **☰** menu instead. This works for everyone, including people who can't open Settings. If they're already on, it says **Notifications are on**.
- **Settings → Notifications.** The **Push notifications** switch does the same, and shows your status: **Enabled**, **Disabled**, **Permission denied** or **Not supported on this browser**.

On a company's own [branded web address](/guides/white-label), there's no Notifications section in Settings, so use the prompt or the account menu.

<Note>
  **On iPhone or iPad**, notifications only work once Paige is on your Home Screen (iOS 16.4 or later). Instead of an **Enable** button, the prompt and the menu show you how: tap **Share**, then **Add to Home Screen**, and open Paige from there. Then turn notifications on from the installed app. See [Install it](#install-it).
</Note>

### What Paige notifies you about

Paige notifies you about four things. Tapping a notification opens Paige in the right place: the customer's chat for a message, or the Code tab for a deploy.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="A customer message that needs you" icon="message-circle">
    Titled with the contact's name, showing their message. You get this when a conversation is [in handover](/concepts/conversations) — you've taken over and they've replied — or when someone starts a **new conversation** after 24 hours of silence.

    You won't get pinged for every message your bot handles by itself. That's the point: it's telling you when your bot *isn't* handling it.

    Everyone with access to Conversations on the project gets these, except that someone whose [conversation access](/guides/settings-project#conversation-access) is **Only assigned** only gets them for the conversations assigned to them.

    **If your bot runs on your own server**, it works differently: only the person the chat is assigned to is told when the customer replies, and only while the chat is in handover. The notification shows the contact's name and the start of their message. It stays quiet for messages the bot handles on its own, and never tells you about your own replies.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A conversation was assigned to you" icon="user-check">
    **New conversation assigned**, with the contact's name, when a manager [assigns you a conversation](/guides/conversations-tab#assigning-conversations). Only the person it's assigned to gets this.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Your deploy finished" icon="rocket">
    **Build succeeded** or **Build failed**, naming the project. Only the person who clicked Deploy gets this.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A broadcast changed state" icon="megaphone">
    **Campaign sending** when it starts, **Campaign sent** when it finishes, and **Campaign needs attention** if it was cancelled because Meta didn't approve its template in time. Only the person who created it gets these.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### Per device, but every device

Turning notifications on affects **this device only** — turning them on on your phone doesn't turn them on on your laptop. Signing out turns them off on that device.

But once a device is subscribed it gets everything. So with your phone and laptop both on, every notification arrives on both.

### While Paige is open in front of you

You still get a notification about a chat, unless you're looking at that exact chat. So with Paige open on one customer's chat, a message from another customer still shows a notification, and a message on the chat you have open doesn't: you see it arrive on screen. Click the notification and that chat opens in the Paige window you already have.

Deploy and broadcast notifications stay quiet while Paige is open and in front of you. They show up when Paige is in the background, on another tab, or closed.

### Alert sound

Paige also plays a short sound in your browser when a chat is assigned to you, and when a customer messages a chat assigned to you that isn't open on your screen. It's on by default.

To turn it off, click your picture at the top right and untick **Alert sound**. On a phone it's a switch in the **☰** menu, and it's also in **Settings → Notifications**. The setting is for that browser only, and it works whether or not notifications are on.

- **No sound on the first alert?** Browsers only let a page play sound after you've clicked on it, so click anywhere in Paige once after opening it. Some phones don't allow it at all; you still get the notification.
- **Fewer sounds than messages?** That's on purpose. A run of messages from one person plays one sound every 30 seconds, and when many chats are assigned to you at once you hear one a minute.
- **Which project?** The sound only plays for the project you have open. Another project's chats still get a notification.
- If your access is **Only assigned**, there's no sound or notification for chats you can't see.

Push notifications aren't the only heads-up Paige can give you. You can also ask for an **email** in any month your bot uses up its 1,000 free WhatsApp service messages. That one is switched on per project in **Tools → Message Costs**, in the **Email reminder** card, not here, and it's for bots hosted by Paige only. See [Get an email when the free messages run out](/guides/message-costs#get-an-email-when-the-free-messages-run-out).

### If notifications are blocked

If you've blocked notifications for Paige, Paige can't ask again. The account menu reads **Notifications are blocked** and tells you what to do: in a browser, use the icon next to the web address to set Notifications to **Allow**, then reload the page. In the installed app, open your device's settings, find Paige under Notifications and allow them, then reopen Paige.

If you don't see a way to turn notifications on at all, your browser can't receive them.

## Offline

Paige needs a connection — it's a live view of a bot running on a server. Lose signal and you'll get a **You're offline** screen with a **Try again** button.
