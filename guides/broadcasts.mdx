Your bot answers people who message it. **Broadcasts** are the other direction: reaching out to many customers at once with an approved template — a seasonal promotion, a reminder that bookings open Monday, a notice that you're closed next week.

You'll find **Broadcasts** as a tab at the top of your project, next to Conversations.

<PageDemo src="/assets/videos/broadcasts.mp4" poster="/assets/videos/broadcasts.poster.jpg" />

<Note>
  Broadcasts needs turning on for a project before the tab appears, from **Settings → Project**. It's also a paid feature, so it's locked once your trial ends.
</Note>

## Before you start

You need two things:

1. **An approved template.** WhatsApp doesn't let businesses send whatever they like to people who haven't messaged recently — every broadcast goes out as a [template](/guides/templates) that Meta has reviewed and approved. Approval takes minutes for a utility template and can take hours for a marketing one, so do this first.
2. **Contacts to send to.** Either from people who've messaged your bot, or [imported in bulk](/guides/contacts-import).

## Building a broadcast

Click **New Broadcast** and you'll go through six steps.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Template">
    Name the broadcast — for you, not your customers — and pick your template. Only **approved** templates appear here.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Variables">
    If your template has `{{1}}`-style placeholders, fill each one. Two choices per placeholder:

    - **Static text** — the same words for everyone.
    - **Contact field** — each person's own **Full name**, **Phone number**, or **Username**.

    A contact field is what turns one template into a personal message. The preview below shows how it'll read.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Audience">
    Either pick a [saved segment](/guides/broadcasts/segments), or build a one-off filter here from tags. The tag box lists the tags your contacts already carry with a count beside each, so you can see what you're about to reach before you pick — see [Tags](/guides/broadcasts/segments#building-one). **Opted in only** is on by default — leave it that way.

    A live count tells you how many people you're about to reach. If it says zero, you can't continue.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Schedule">
    Pick a date and time, or save it as a draft to come back to. Two switches also live here:

    - **Send in batches (waves)** — set how many to **send now** and Paige holds the rest back for you to release later (see [Sending in batches](#sending-in-batches-waves)).
    - **Auto-retarget quality-limit failures** — off by default. Turn it on and Paige automatically tries the recipients WhatsApp quality-blocked again about 24 hours later (see [Auto-retargeting quality-limit failures](#auto-retargeting-quality-limit-failures)).

    See below — this step matters more than it looks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test Send">
    Send the real template to up to three numbers first. It doesn't touch your audience or your daily cap, and it's the only way to see the thing exactly as a customer will. Worth the thirty seconds.

    A test that sends successfully also **shows up in that number's thread in the Conversations tab**, so you can look at exactly what the person received rather than trusting the preview.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Review it all, including the estimated WhatsApp cost, and save.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Launching and scheduling

You send a broadcast one of two ways:

- **Launch it now.** A finished draft has a **Launch** action in the broadcast list — click it, confirm, and the broadcast goes out straight away.
- **Schedule it.** Give it a date and time on the Schedule step and Paige launches it for you when that time comes.

<Warning>
  **A draft never sends on its own.** If you only **Save as draft** and don't launch or schedule it, it sits there indefinitely — no matter how long you leave it. To send it, either click **Launch** on it in the list, or open it and set a schedule.
</Warning>

## Sending in batches (waves)

If you have a large audience and want to test the waters first, switch on **Send in batches (waves)** on the wizard's Schedule step and choose how many to **send now**. When the broadcast launches, Paige **freezes the whole audience** and sends only that first batch — everyone else is put on hold. Your counters still cover the full audience on the one broadcast, so you never have to stitch several broadcasts together.

The broadcast then shows as **Partially sent** rather than jumping to Completed. Open it and a panel tells you how many are still held — **200 recipients remaining**, say — with a **Send now** / **Schedule** toggle for releasing them.

Release as many waves as you like, whenever you like. Each time, anyone who has **opted out** since you launched is skipped automatically. The broadcast only marks itself **Completed** once the last held recipient has been released and finished sending.

### Releasing a wave now

Leave the toggle on **Send now**, then either:

- Type how many to release and click the send button, which counts back what you typed — **Send 50 more**.
- Or click **Send all remaining** to release everyone left.

### Scheduling a wave for later

Switch the toggle to **Schedule** and you get two fields instead:

- **How many** — leave it blank to schedule everyone remaining (the placeholder reads `All`), or type a number.
- **Send at (your local time)** — the date and time to release it. It has to be in the future.

The submit button counts back what you typed — **Schedule 50** — or reads **Schedule all remaining** when you left the count blank. Paige notes underneath that it *fires within ~15 minutes of the chosen time*: the release runs on a fifteen-minute cycle, so treat the time you pick as "no earlier than", not to the second.

Once scheduled, the controls are replaced by a banner — **50 scheduled for 14 Aug 2026, 09:00**, or **All remaining scheduled for** that time — with the note that it *releases automatically around this time (within ~15 minutes)* and that opted-out contacts are skipped at release.

To change a pending schedule, just schedule again: setting a new count or time overwrites the previous one. There's no separate edit control.

<Warning>
  **Cancel schedule** is not **Cancel**. **Cancel schedule** only clears the timer — the held recipients stay held, and you can still release them by hand or schedule them again. **Cancel** stops the whole broadcast and can't be undone. See [Cancelling and retrying](#cancelling-and-retrying).
</Warning>

Scheduling a wave is only offered while a broadcast is **Sending** or **Partially sent** and still has recipients held back. It isn't available on self-hosted projects.

<Note>
  The audience is fixed at launch. Contacts who join it *after* you launch aren't added to later waves — to reach new people, create a fresh broadcast.
</Note>

Batched sending is available on standard Paige-hosted projects. Projects running their own self-hosted backend send everyone at once, and the toggle is hidden.

## What it costs

Paige doesn't charge for broadcasts, and they don't use your credits. **Meta bills your connected number directly** for the messages, and the estimate on the Confirm step is a rough guide rather than a quote.

The one limit is a **daily message cap on each project**, which starts at **1,000 messages a day** and resets at midnight UTC. It's a per-project setting rather than a fixed platform ceiling, so a project can be raised above the default. There's no self-service control for it — ask the [Help Agent](/agents/help-agent) if 1,000 a day isn't enough for you.

You can see where you stand without doing the arithmetic: the Broadcasts tab always shows how much of today's allowance you've used, and the wizard's **Confirm** step tells you how much is left before you commit. If your audience is bigger than the remainder, you know up front that the overflow goes out the next day.

### When a broadcast hits the cap

A broadcast that runs out of allowance mid-send **pauses visibly and picks up where it left off**. You get a notification, its badge changes from *Sending* to **Paused — daily limit**, and a banner on both the broadcast list and the broadcast itself says how many messages you've used and the exact time the rest will start going out.

Nothing is lost — the held recipients are still queued, and they go out on their own when the allowance resets. There's nothing to click.

<Note>
  The cap is per **project**, not per broadcast. When one broadcast pauses, every other broadcast in that project is waiting too.
</Note>

<Note>
  Self-hosted projects don't show the allowance or the forecast. Their sending goes through their own backend, so Paige isn't the one counting.
</Note>

## Who actually receives it

Two groups are always excluded, whatever your filter says:

- **Anyone who opted out.** This is checked when the broadcast sends, not when you build it — so someone who sends STOP after you schedule is still excluded.
- **Numbers confirmed not to be on WhatsApp.**

That second one is worth understanding. Paige doesn't know whether an imported number is on WhatsApp until it tries. Your first broadcast to fresh imports is what finds out: the dead ones fail once with **Not on WhatsApp**, and they're quietly left out of every audience after that. A first broadcast with more failures than you expected usually means exactly this, and it's self-correcting.

## Watching it go out

Open a broadcast to see live counters updating as it goes. **Total** is the size of the whole audience, and next to it sit six status tiles: **Queued**, **Sent**, **Delivered**, **Read**, **Failed**, and **Canceled**.

<Note>
  Those six are buckets, not totals. Each person sits in exactly one, and they move along: Sent → Delivered → Read. So a broadcast everyone has read shows **Sent: 0**. That's success, not failure.
</Note>

There's no "Held" tile. If you [sent in batches](#sending-in-batches-waves), the recipients still waiting are shown in the wave panel below the tiles — **200 recipients remaining** — and that's the number to look for, not one of the six.

Below that, every recipient is listed with their status. Failures say why in plain language:

| What you'll see | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Not on WhatsApp** | The number isn't reachable. Won't be tried again. |
| **Rate limited — can retry** | WhatsApp throttled you. Worth retrying. |
| **Blocked by quality limits** | Meta has restricted your number's sending, usually after too many blocks or reports, or you've hit a per-user messaging limit. This is the one bucket [auto-retargeting](#auto-retargeting-quality-limit-failures) can retry for you. |
| **Template rejected** | Meta withdrew approval for the template. |
| **Account paused** | Meta has paused your WhatsApp account. |
| **Delivery failed** | Something else. Copy the raw reason for the detail. |

## Downloading the recipient list

Next to the filter is a **Download CSV** button. It saves the recipient list as a spreadsheet you can open in Excel or Google Sheets, or feed into another system — each person's name, number, WhatsApp status, failure reason if any, the wave they went out in, how many attempts were made, and the times things happened.

It respects the filter you have on. With **Failed** selected you get only the failures, which is the quickest way to work through bad numbers offline. Phone numbers keep their leading `+` rather than being mangled into plain numbers by the spreadsheet.

The file is a snapshot of that exact moment — deliveries and reads keep arriving from WhatsApp for a while after a send — so the filename carries the date and time, and you can download again later for an updated picture.

<Note>
  **Download CSV** is shown to the project owner and workspace admins only, because the file contains everyone's contact details. If you're a member and don't see the button, that's why, not a bug.
</Note>

Two cases where you won't get a file:

- **A broadcast that hasn't started sending.** There's no recipient list yet to export.
- **A broadcast that failed before it built its list.** You get a short explanation instead. An empty spreadsheet would look like nobody was in your audience, when in fact the send never got started.

Self-hosted projects keep their recipient list on their own backend rather than in Paige, so the download isn't offered there.

## Exporting several broadcasts at once

For a monthly report, you don't need to open each campaign. On the Broadcasts list every row has a tick box: tick the campaigns you want and click **Export**. A window asks what to include.

- **Campaign summary** — one spreadsheet row per campaign with its status, template, audience, every count (total, queued, sent, delivered, read, failed, canceled, held), the estimated cost, and all the timestamps.
- **Recipient detail** — a ZIP containing one recipient list per campaign (the same file **Download CSV** gives you), plus the summary if you ticked it, plus a short README.

<Warning>
  Two columns in the summary are named `estimated_credits_by_audience` and `estimated_credits_by_messages_sent`. Despite the name these are **not Paige credits being charged** — they're the same estimate of Meta's messaging fee described under [What it costs](#what-it-costs), one priced against everyone you targeted and one against the messages that actually went out.
</Warning>

You can tick up to **25 broadcasts** at a time, and there's a ceiling on the total recipients one export may cover. Go over either and the window tells you, so you untick a few.

Campaigns that haven't started sending still appear in the summary but contribute no recipient list, because there is nothing to list. The window names them before you download and the README names them again.

Like the single download, bulk export is owner and workspace-admin only. On a self-hosted project the ZIP arrives with the summary and the README but no recipient files, and the README says so for each campaign — you still get a usable report, it just stops at the campaign level.

## Cancelling and retrying

**Cancel** appears on a broadcast that is **Scheduled** or **Sending**, and stops it. Anything already sent has left and can't be recalled — cancelling only stops what's still queued, including anyone held back for a later wave. It can't be undone.

You'll be asked to confirm, and the confirmation counts exactly who is affected — the queued recipients plus any held for a later wave. Since cancelling is final, and sending the same campaign again means building a new broadcast, it's worth reading those numbers before you go ahead.

<Note>
  Don't confuse this with **Cancel schedule** in the wave panel, which only clears a pending wave release and leaves the broadcast alone. See [Scheduling a wave for later](#scheduling-a-wave-for-later).
</Note>

**Retry failures** appears once a broadcast has finished with failures. It re-queues only the ones worth retrying — a rate-limited send, say — and skips the permanent ones like *Not on WhatsApp* or *Template rejected*, since retrying those would just fail again.

## Auto-retargeting quality-limit failures

WhatsApp sometimes blocks part of a broadcast because of your number's quality rating, or because a particular person has already received too many messages from you. Those recipients show as **Blocked by quality limits**, and they're usually reachable again once a day has passed.

**Auto-retarget quality-limit failures** on the wizard's Schedule step does that for you. It's **off by default**. The switch explains itself in-product:

> If WhatsApp blocks some recipients for quality or per-user messaging limits, automatically try them again about 24 hours later. You can cancel the retry any time before it sends.

Here's what actually happens when it's on:

- About **24 hours** after the original broadcast finishes, Paige creates a follow-up broadcast aimed at exactly the recipients WhatsApp quality-blocked.
- It uses the same template and the same variable settings as the original.
- The follow-up appears in your broadcast list under the original's name with **(auto-retarget)** on the end — *Winter sale (auto-retarget)* — in **Scheduled** status. You can open it, check it, and **Cancel** it like any other scheduled broadcast before it sends.

<Note>
  It retries **only** the quality-limit bucket. Numbers that aren't on WhatsApp, a rejected template, or any other failure are never retargeted — retrying those would just fail the same way. For rate-limited sends, use **Retry failures** instead.
</Note>

Three limits worth knowing:

- **It happens once.** A retarget is never itself retargeted, so you can't end up in a retry loop.
- **It's capped at 500 contacts.** If more than 500 recipients were quality-blocked, the follow-up covers the first 500.
- **Paige waits before it looks.** WhatsApp reports quality failures asynchronously — a message that looked sent can flip to failed minutes later — so Paige leaves about **2 hours** after the original completes before working out who to retarget. The follow-up still sends around 24 hours after the original finished.

The switch is hidden on self-hosted projects, which manage their own recipient list and so can't be retargeted from Paige.

## Opting out (STOP) and back in (START)

Your bot handles opt-outs on its own. If someone replies **STOP**, **UNSUBSCRIBE**, **OPT OUT**, or **OPTOUT**, Paige marks them opted out immediately, before the message reaches your bot. They're excluded from every broadcast from then on — including ones you've already scheduled, because the check happens at send time.

The match is case-insensitive and forgiving of extra spaces and punctuation, so "stop", " STOP. " and "Unsubscribe" all work. But the word has to *start* the message and stand on its own, so "please don't stop messaging me" won't trigger it.

Your customer gets no confirmation reply when they opt out — they send STOP and hear nothing back. That's worth knowing if someone asks whether it worked.

### Coming back

Someone who opted out by mistake, or who later changes their mind, can **re-subscribe themselves** by replying **START** — or **UNSTOP**, **SUBSCRIBE**, or **OPT IN**. They're marked opted in again, they get a short confirmation reply this time, and they're back in future audiences as normal. The same rules apply: the word has to begin the message and stand alone, so "can we start tomorrow?" and "startup" go to your bot as ordinary messages.

<Note>
  **Only the contact can do this, by messaging you.** There's deliberately no button for you to opt someone back in on their behalf — someone asking to be re-subscribed should send START themselves, which is simpler for you and the version that keeps you on the right side of WhatsApp's rules.
</Note>

<Warning>
  **Re-importing a contact does not opt them back in.** Imports skip numbers you already have and never overwrite an opt-out. See [Import contacts](/guides/contacts-import).
</Warning>

<Note>
  This applies to bots Paige hosts. If you run your own backend, opt-out handling is yours to implement.
</Note>
