Connecting a WhatsApp Business number is the step that takes your bot live. It runs through Meta — the company that owns WhatsApp — so most of this page is about Meta's side of the process: what Meta Business Suite is, how business verification works, why Meta needs a payment method from you, and what you'll see as you click through Meta's signup. Paige launches that signup from inside your dashboard and handles the technical wiring (the API token and webhooks) for you, so you never leave Paige and never touch code.

<Note>
  New to the WhatsApp Business Platform? Read [Which WhatsApp product, and when](/learn/whatsapp-products) first. It explains why the Platform — the programmable version Paige builds on — needs a Meta business account and a dedicated number in the first place. This page assumes you've decided the Platform is right for you.
</Note>

## What you'll need

- A **Facebook account**. Meta uses it to identify you and tie your business assets together. It doesn't need a Facebook Page or any posts — it's just the login Meta recognises you by.
- A **phone number** for WhatsApp. It can be a mobile or landline number, but it **must not** already be running as a WhatsApp account — personal or Business App — on a phone. Many businesses use a fresh number to avoid disruption.
- A few **business details** — your legal business name, address, website, and (for verification) a document or two that proves the business is real. More on that below.

<Warning>
  If the number you want is currently a personal WhatsApp account, delete that account in the WhatsApp app before you register it here. That step can't be undone — you'll lose the chat history on it.
</Warning>

## Meta Business Suite, in one minute

The WhatsApp Business Platform isn't a standalone app you sign up for — it lives inside **Meta Business Suite** (you may also see it called Meta Business Manager), the same place businesses manage Facebook Pages, Instagram, and ad accounts. Meta groups everything a business owns — including your **WhatsApp Business Account**, or **WABA** — under one business portfolio there.

Two terms are worth knowing before you start, because they show up throughout signup:

- **Business portfolio** — the top-level container for everything your business owns in Meta. You'll create one during signup if you don't have one.
- **WhatsApp Business Account (WABA)** — the asset inside that portfolio that holds your phone number(s) and messaging settings. One WABA can hold several numbers; each Paige project connects to one number.

You don't need to set any of this up in advance. Meta's embedded signup — the flow Paige launches — creates the portfolio and the WABA for you as you go. Knowing the words just makes the screens easier to follow.

## Verifying your business

Meta wants to know a real business is behind a number. **Business verification** is Meta confirming that your business exists, using documents such as a company registration certificate or a utility bill. You do it in Meta Business Manager, separately from connecting your number.

**Verification is what lets you publish WhatsApp Flows.** Meta only allows verified businesses to publish [flows](/guides/flows/overview) — the interactive forms and menus your bot sends. An unverified business can create and edit flows in Paige and test them on Paige Dev, but those flows won't go live on your own number. Verification also lifts the tighter limits an unverified number runs under: a lower cap on how many new people you can message a day, and a stricter path to a custom display name.

**You can connect your number and start messaging before you're verified.** An unverified number sends and receives ordinary messages. So the usual order is: connect your number now, start building and testing, and submit for verification in parallel — well before you need your first flow live.

<Accordion title="What Meta asks for" icon="file-check">
  Verification confirms your business's legal name, address, and phone number against official records, so what you enter must match your paperwork exactly. The shape of the request is always the same: a document that proves the legal entity exists, and a document that proves its address.

  Which documents Meta accepts depends on your country and changes over time, so check [Meta's guide to verifying your business](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2058515294227817) for the current list before you submit.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How long it takes" icon="clock">
  Meta usually takes a few days to review your verification, though it can be faster or slower depending on your documents and region. You submit once and wait for Meta's decision — there's nothing to do in Paige while it's in review.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What you can and can't do while waiting" icon="hourglass">
  You **can** connect your number, build, test your flows on [Paige Dev](/quickstart), deploy your code and tables, and message customers within the daily limit of an unverified number. You **can't** publish a flow to your own number, raise those limits, or lock in a fully custom display name until verification clears.

  Nothing you build is wasted. Your flows are saved as drafts on your WhatsApp account while you wait, and they go live the next time you deploy after verification comes through.
</Accordion>

### Starting verification from Paige

Paige shows your **Business Verification** status in **Settings → WhatsApp**, under your number. When Meta reports that your business isn't verified, a **Get verified** button appears next to the status. It opens Meta Business Manager in a new tab, where you start or finish verification.

In Meta Business Manager, verification sits under your business portfolio's **Business settings → Security Centre**. Meta moves and renames these screens from time to time, so follow what's on screen rather than this exact path.

### When verification holds your flows back

A deploy doesn't fail because your business isn't verified. It does everything it can, then tells you what it couldn't:

- Your **code and tables go live** as normal.
- Each flow is still **saved as a draft** on your WhatsApp account. Paige skips the publish step and tells you verification is the reason, rather than failing with a confusing error.
- A flow that was **already live stays on its current live version**. Nothing is taken off the air.
- You get **one warning** — "flows waiting on business verification" — listing the flows it held back. It stays on screen until you close it, and its **Get verified** button takes you to **Settings → WhatsApp**.
- It still counts as your **first deploy** in the getting-started checklist, because the rest of the deploy did go live.

Clicking **Publish** on a flow in the Flows tab tells you the same thing.

Deploying again won't change the result until verification is done. Finish business verification in Meta Business Manager, then click **Deploy** again — Paige doesn't publish for you the moment verification comes through. In the meantime you can keep testing your flows on the Paige Dev number, which isn't affected.

## Adding a payment method on Meta

Meta charges for some conversations: [broadcasts](/guides/broadcasts), and other messages your business starts. Those messages fail to send if no payment method is attached to your business in Meta. The charges are Meta's, billed against your own connected number — they're separate from what you pay for Paige, and nothing comes out of your Paige credits. See [Billing](/guides/billing) for the Paige side.

You add the payment method in Meta Business Suite, under your WhatsApp Business Account's payment settings. Paige gives you a short cut: **Settings → WhatsApp** shows a payment-method reminder with a **Check payment method** button that opens your Meta payment settings in a new tab.

<Note>
  **Seeing that reminder doesn't mean anything is wrong.** Meta gives Paige no reliable way to check whether a card is on file, so rather than guess, Paige shows the reminder either way. It's there so you can confirm it yourself.
</Note>

## Your business info: display name and category

Two pieces of business info shape how you appear to customers, and both have downstream effects worth understanding before you pick them.

**Display name** — the name customers see at the top of the chat. Meta reviews every display name against your verified business and its own [display name guidelines](/whatsapp-setup#display-name-approval), so it should be your real brand name, not a generic word like "Support." A name that doesn't match your verified business is the most common rejection. Until verification and name approval complete, your number can still send — it just shows a less-polished name.

**Category** — the industry that best describes your business (retail, hospitality, professional services, and so on). It sets how your business is classified in Meta's systems and appears on your business profile. Pick the closest honest match; a wildly off category can slow verification.

<Note>
  You set your final display name, profile photo, and business profile details inside Paige after connecting — see [Update your business profile](/whatsapp-setup#update-your-business-profile) below. During signup, just give Meta a sensible business name to start with.
</Note>

## Connect your number

You connect WhatsApp from inside your project. Open your project, go to **Settings**, and select the **WhatsApp** tab in the sidebar — that's `/projects/<your-project-id>?tab=settings&section=whatsapp` if you want to link straight to it. Everything below happens on that one screen. (If your bot isn't connected yet, you'll also see a **Connect** button in the project header that jumps you here.)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the WhatsApp tab in your project">
    In your project, go to **Settings → WhatsApp**. When no number is connected, the tab shows a **Connect your WhatsApp number** panel: "Before we hand off to Meta's setup, confirm you have both of these ready." Tick both boxes:

    - **I have a Meta Business Suite account.**
    - **I have a phone number that can receive an OTP.** An OTP is the one-time code Meta sends to prove you control the number.

    The **Connect WhatsApp** button appears only once both boxes are ticked. Until then it isn't greyed out — it isn't on the screen at all. Helper text under it reads "You'll be guided through Meta's setup to connect your WhatsApp Business account."

### You'll get a confirmation email

The first time a number is connected to a project, Paige emails the project owner — or the workspace owner, for a workspace project — to confirm the connection and to flag the two things on Meta's side that can hold you up later.

- If Meta reports your business **isn't verified** yet, the email tells you to start business verification, because Meta won't let you publish flows until it's done and it usually takes a few days.
- If your business is **already verified**, the email says so.
- If Paige **couldn't check**, the email explains that flows need verification and where to look.
- Either way, it reminds you to make sure a **payment method** is attached to your WhatsApp Business Account, because Meta charges for broadcasts.

Every link in the email takes you to **Settings → WhatsApp**.

<Note>
  You only get this email once per number. Disconnect a number and connect the same one again and no second copy arrives — but connecting a different number sends a new one.
</Note>

<Note>
  Connecting a WhatsApp number is available during your free trial **and** after it ends — you can finish setup whenever you're ready. Once a number is connected, your bot runs on it while you have an active subscription or an unexpired trial. See [Billing](/guides/billing) for what the trial includes.
</Note>

## Understanding the connection details

Once connected, the WhatsApp tab shows what Meta reports about your account. Under **WhatsApp Business Account** you'll see:

- **Account Name** — the name on your WhatsApp Business Account.
- **Status** — the account's standing with Meta.
- **Business Verification** — whether Meta has verified your business. Your flows only publish to this number once it says verified (see [Verifying your business](/whatsapp-setup#verifying-your-business)).
- **Currency** and **Timezone** — the account's billing currency and time zone, set from your Meta business settings.

The technical identifiers behind the scenes — your WABA ID, phone number ID, and access token — are handled by Paige and kept securely on the server. You don't need them for day-to-day use, and Paige manages token storage and refresh automatically.

## Update your business profile

After connecting, **Settings → WhatsApp** is where you shape what customers see when they open a chat with you. It's laid out in two columns.

### On the left: your number and its health

- **Profile photo** — your logo, via **Upload Photo**. JPEG or PNG.
- **Phone number** — the connected number. Read-only.
- **Display name** — the name customers see. Edit it and click **Request Change**; Meta has to approve it before it takes effect. A **Status** badge under the field tracks where the request stands, and reads `PENDING` while Meta reviews. See [Display name approval](/whatsapp-setup#display-name-approval) below.

Below those, four numbers Meta reports about your number. They're worth glancing at now and then, because they're how you find out you're in trouble before your customers do:

| | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Quality Rating** | How customers are reacting to your messages. **Green** is healthy; **yellow** means Meta has noticed blocks or reports; **red** means your sending is at risk. |
| **Throughput** | How fast you can send — Standard is 80 messages a second, High is 1,000. |
| **Messaging Limit** | How many *different* customers you can start a conversation with in 24 hours. Starts at 1,000 and rises as your quality stays good. Relevant when planning a [broadcast](/guides/broadcasts). |
| **Business Verification** | Whether Meta has verified your business. Unverified numbers face tighter limits, and their flows won't publish. A **Get verified** button sits next to it when Meta reports you're not verified. |

<Tip>
  A yellow or red quality rating is a signal to slow down and look at what you're sending. Ratings fall when people block or report you — usually after a broadcast to an audience that didn't expect it. Left alone, it costs you your messaging limit and eventually your ability to send.
</Tip>

Underneath those four, you'll also see a **payment-method reminder** with a **Check payment method** button. It appears whether or not a card is on file, so it isn't a fault report — see [Adding a payment method on Meta](/whatsapp-setup#adding-a-payment-method-on-meta).

### On the right: your business profile

Six fields, all under **Business Profile**:

- **About** — a short line on your profile, up to 139 characters.
- **Description** — a fuller description, up to 512 characters.
- **Address**
- **Email**
- **Websites** — up to two.
- **Industry** — pick the closest match from Meta's list.

Click **Save Profile** when you're done. These take effect immediately — only the display name needs review.

### Display name approval

When you request a display name change, Meta reviews the new name against your business verification status and brand guidelines. This process can take from a few hours to several days.

<Note>
  During review, your existing display name remains active. Users won't see the new name until Meta approves it. You'll receive an update in the Paige dashboard once the review is complete.
</Note>

Common reasons Meta rejects display name requests:

- The name doesn't match the verified business name on your WABA
- The name uses a generic term without a brand identifier (for example, "Support" on its own)
- The name implies an official relationship with Meta or WhatsApp

If your request is rejected, you can update your business verification details in Meta Business Manager and submit a revised name.

## Claim a WhatsApp username

WhatsApp lets a business claim a **username** — an `@handle` customers can find you by without knowing your number. It's in the **Username** field in Settings → WhatsApp.

Type the handle you want and click **Claim**. If you already have one, the button reads **Change** instead, and **Remove** gives it up.

Your username has a status:

- **reserved** — it's yours, waiting on Meta.
- **approved** — live.

<Note>
  Usernames are globally unique across all of WhatsApp, and a number can hold one at a time. Meta decides who gets what — Paige just passes your request along, so a name can be refused for reasons outside Paige's control.
</Note>

If you haven't claimed one, Paige suggests handles based on your brand — click one to claim it directly.

If the handle you want is already on **another of your own numbers**, you'll be told so and offered **Transfer & claim**, which moves it across. It comes off the other number in the process.

## Share your number

The **Share Your WhatsApp** card — under **Settings → Project** — gives customers a way in that isn't typing a phone number:

- A **QR code** they scan to open a chat. On your own connected number, **Download PNG** saves it for a poster, a receipt, or a shop window.
- A **wa.me link** to put on your website or in an email signature. **Copy** it.
- A **Pre-filled Message** so the chat opens with text already typed — "Hi, I'd like to book a table" — which gives your bot something to work with from the first message.
- **Share** buttons for WhatsApp, LinkedIn, and Instagram, again on your own connected number only. Instagram has no share API, so that one copies the link for you to paste.

Once you have your own number connected, the card gets **Connected** and **Paige Dev** tabs so you can switch between the two. If you haven't connected a number yet, it shows the **Paige Dev** test number on its own, so you can still scan and try your bot.

<Note>
  **Download PNG** and the **Share** row are hidden on the **Paige Dev** tab — they only appear for your own connected number. The QR code, the wa.me link, and the pre-filled message all still work there. That Paige Dev QR is for testing only; don't put it in front of customers.
</Note>

## Troubleshooting

**The Meta popup closes without completing** — This usually means a browser extension (such as an ad blocker or popup blocker) interrupted the flow. Disable extensions for the Paige domain and try again.

**My phone number verification code isn't arriving** — Make sure the number can actually receive whichever method Meta offered you, and that it isn't already registered as a WhatsApp personal account. If you've requested multiple codes, wait at least 10 minutes before requesting another.

**Signup ended in an error** — A red banner appears above the checkbox card showing Meta's error, or "Something went wrong. Please try again." when no detail came back. The card stays on screen with your ticks intact, so retrying is just clicking **Connect WhatsApp** again — there's no separate retry or re-register control on this tab. If it keeps failing, contact Paige support with your project ID.

**The tab says "Could not load account details"** — In full: "Could not load account details. Check that the WhatsApp Business Account is properly configured." Your number is connected — Paige just couldn't read the WhatsApp Business Account details back from Meta. Check in Meta Business Suite that the WABA is fully set up, then reload the tab.
